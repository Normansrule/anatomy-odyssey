    Scientific notation

    Writing 12,742,000 m or 0.000 000 000 000 001 68 m is slow and error-prone, so scientists count the zeros instead. A number is written as a value between 1 and 10 times a power of ten:

    A positive power counts places to the left of the decimal point, a negative one places to the right. Each step up the ruler multiplies by ten, so the whole journey from a proton (10−15 m) to the observable universe (1027 m) takes 42 steps.

    The view right now, written out in full—in scientific notation—

    Prefixes do the same job in words: femto (f) = 10−15, pico (p) = 10−12, nano (n) = 10−9, micro (µ) = 10−6, milli (m) = 10−3, kilo (k) = 103.

    Why the observable universe is 93 billion light-years across

    The universe is 13.8 billion years old, so you might expect to see 13.8 billion light-years in every direction. But space has been expanding the whole time. Light that set out long ago crossed regions that have since stretched, so the places it came from are now much farther away than the time it travelled suggests.

    How this page is drawn

    Every object is sized from the number in its card, in metres, and drawn procedurally in your browser: no images are downloaded. Objects are placed side by side in order of size so that neighbours can be compared; their positions are arranged for comparison and are not where they really are (except within systems like the planets). The background stars and particles are decoration that moves with the zoom.

    The light-time is simply the size divided by the speed of light, c = 299,792,458 m/s.
