1 · Isaac Newton's thought experiment

Why orbiting is just falling

Newton imagined a mountain so tall its summit rises above the air. Fire a cannonball sideways and it falls, but Earth's surface curves away beneath it. Fire it fast enough and the ground drops away exactly as fast as the ball falls: it never lands. That is an orbit.

The sign of ε decides the fate: ε < 0 is a closed (bound) ellipse, ε = 0 is a parabola that just escapes, ε > 0 is a hyperbola. At sea level (with no air) the thresholds are 7.91 and 11.19 km/s; from the summit they are slightly lower because gravity is weaker up there.

2 · Johannes Kepler, 1609 and 1619

Three laws, one ellipse

  1. Ellipses. A planet moves on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus.
  2. Equal areas. The line from the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal times, so the planet races at perihelion and dawdles at aphelion.
  3. Harmony. The square of the period grows as the cube of the size.
How this is computed

4 · The gravity assist (slingshot)

Borrowing speed from Jupiter

In Jupiter's frame the flyby is perfectly symmetric: the spacecraft leaves exactly as fast as it arrived; only its direction turns. But Jupiter itself is racing around the Sun. Add Jupiter's velocity back in (Sun's frame) and that turn becomes a real change of speed.

Hyperbola
Frame change
Energy gained

Pass behind Jupiter (its trailing side) and the bend swings your velocity toward Jupiter's motion: you speed up. Pass ahead and you slow down, which is how probes fall inward toward the Sun. Momentum is conserved: Jupiter slows by about 10−24 km/s. Missions: Pioneer 10 and 11, Voyager 1 and 2, Galileo, Cassini–Huygens, New Horizons (+≈4 km/s at Jupiter in 2007).

How this is computed
  • The spacecraft is placed on the exact incoming hyperbola 40 Jupiter radii out (or further for wide passes) and integrated through the flyby with leapfrog (kick–drift–kick) using a step of 0.4 % of r/v.
  • The Sun-frame path is the planet-frame path plus Jupiter's straight-line motion, the patched-conic approximation: inside Jupiter's sphere of influence (≈ 48 million km) the Sun's pull is ignored.
  • Speeds before/after use the asymptotic (v∞) values. μJ = 1.26686534 × 10⁸ km³/s², RJ = 71,492 km, VJ = 13.06 km/s (NASA Jupiter Fact Sheet).
5 · Leonhard Euler (1767) and Joseph-Louis Lagrange (1772)

Five places to park

Spin along with two orbiting bodies and there are five points where gravity and the centrifugal effect balance. The colour map is the effective potential of that rotating frame: bright ridges are hilltops, dark basins are gravity wells.

Effective potential
Rotating-frame motion
Jacobi constant

L1, L2, L3 are saddle points: a particle placed there drifts away within a few orbits, so spacecraft need regular station-keeping burns. L4 and L5 are hilltops, yet the Coriolis effect turns a slide downhill into a loop, so particles circle them in "tadpole" orbits. This works only if μ < 0.0385 (Routh's criterion). Jupiter's Trojan asteroids live at its L4 and L5. The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) orbits Sun–Earth L2, 1.5 million km from Earth; the Solar and Heliospheric Observatory (SOHO) watches the Sun from L1.

How this is computed
  • This is the circular restricted three-body problem (CR3BP), in units where the separation, total mass and angular speed are 1. The Coriolis term makes a velocity-dependent force, so particles are integrated in the non-rotating frame with leapfrog (the two bodies on known circular paths), then rotated into the co-rotating frame for display.
  • The step shrinks near either body, Δt = min(0.002, 0.01·√(d³/m)), one hundredth of the local orbital time scale. The Jacobi constant C readout is a built-in accuracy check: it should hold steady.
  • L1–L3 come from solving ∂Ω/∂x = 0 on the x-axis by bisection; L4 and L5 sit at the tips of equilateral triangles. Mass ratios: Moon/(Earth+Moon) = 0.012150585 (JPL DE430); (Earth+Moon)/Sun ≈ 3.0404 × 10⁻⁶.
  • The colour field is histogram-equalised in log(Ω − ΩL4), so each view uses the full colour range; the brighter contour lines are the energy levels through L1, L2 and L3.
