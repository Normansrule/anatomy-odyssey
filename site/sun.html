A photon's 170,000-year escape

Energy made in the core cannot fly straight out. In the dense plasma a photon travels a fraction of a millimetre before an electron or ion scatters it in a random direction. After N random steps of length ℓ it is only √N·ℓ from where it started, so crossing a distance R takes N = (R/ℓ)² steps:

A single average ℓ is a teaching simplification. Detailed solar models give a radiative diffusion time of about 170,000 years (Mitalas & Sills 1992, Astrophysical Journal 401, 759); in the outer 30% convection takes over and carries the energy up far faster, in weeks rather than millennia. Once free at the photosphere, light reaches Earth in 8 min 19 s.

Solar Cycle 25

Status (September 2026): declining phase. Solar Cycle 25 began at the December 2019 minimum. NASA and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) announced in October 2024 that the Sun had reached its solar maximum period, and the Sunspot Index and Long-term Solar Observations (SILSO) centre at the Royal Observatory of Belgium later placed the peak of the 13-month smoothed sunspot number at about 161 in October 2024, well above the official forecast of 115.

Since 2025 the smoothed sunspot number has been falling, but big eruptions still happen on the way down: an X5.1 flare on 11 November 2025 launched a Coronal Mass Ejection (CME) at roughly 1,950 km/s. The next minimum is expected around 2030. Polar magnetic fields flip at every maximum, so the full magnetic cycle is about 22 years.

Sources: SILSO / Solar Influences Data Analysis Center (SIDC), “Solar Cycle 25 reached its maximum in October 2024”; NASA–NOAA announcement, 15 Oct 2024; NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (SWPC) solar-cycle progression.
